# Car Game - Product Requirements Document

Status: describes the game exactly as built so far (version 1.28 - Rotor Bridge and Windmill always have a rolling floor, smaller layout for small screens, start menu with logo, first to 5 wins the match, tune-up reduced to top speed, suspension and volume (gravity, tyre bounce, torque, acceleration, rolling resistance and air spin fixed at the tuned values), play against the computer, bendy three-wheelers, instant turn-round, The Circle level, exploding caravans, giant cars, cars face the way they go, trampoline pads and pinball bumpers removed, sound effects, four more special rounds (caravans, teensy cars, bomb trailers, square wheels), Giant Heads special round, Pillars level removed, spawn safety (no car starts inside the ground), reshaped Valley, no replay, special rounds (moon, gale, storm), power-ups, bumpers, moving and crumbling platforms, lower wheels and gentler ramps, tyre bounce units fixed, passenger faces, dust and skid marks, speed lines, screen shake, slow motion and knockout replay, wheel pickups, sudden death, suspension, ice/mud/trampoline surfaces, air spin slider, winner colour fade instead of confetti, bonnet and boot above the wheels, throttle acceleration, saved sliders, floors and side walls, 11 random level types with variations, rotating platforms, bigger wheels, tune-up panel, heavier gravity, bouncy tyres, torque-driven wheels, mid-air rotation, sparks, confetti, dynamic zoom).

## 1. Platform and Delivery

1.1. The game runs in a web browser as a single HTML page (`CarGame/wwwroot/index.html`).
1.2. The graphics are drawn on an HTML5 `<canvas>` with an internal resolution of 1280 x 720 (16:9). The canvas scales to fit the browser window while keeping its aspect ratio.
1.3. The game is hosted in a Visual Studio 2026 solution (`car-game.slnx`) containing one ASP.NET Core project (`CarGame`, net10.0). The project only serves static files (`UseDefaultFiles`, `UseStaticFiles`); it has no server-side game logic.
1.4. The development URL is `http://localhost:5286`.
1.5. The 2D physics engine is Matter.js 0.19.0, loaded from the cdnjs CDN. An internet connection is therefore needed to load the page.

## 2. Players and Controls

2.1. The game is for two players on one keyboard.
2.2. Player 1 controls the blue car. Z rotates its wheels anti-clockwise and X rotates them clockwise.
2.3. Player 2 controls the red car. `,` (comma) rotates its wheels anti-clockwise and `.` (full stop) rotates them clockwise.
2.4. Clockwise wheel rotation drives a car to the right on screen. Anti-clockwise drives it to the left.
2.5. Both wheels of a car are driven.
2.6. Holding a key applies drive torque to both wheels, up to a maximum wheel spin. An equal and opposite reaction torque is applied to the chassis, so accelerating hard lifts the nose and a held key quickly produces a wheelie. Holding it long enough flips the car over backwards. Releasing the key lets the player recover.
2.6a. Mid-air rotation (a gameplay rule, not strict physics): when a car has had no wheel or body contact with the terrain for 80 ms, its keys instead rotate the whole car - anti-clockwise key rotates the car clockwise and the clockwise key rotates it anti-clockwise (deliberately reversed from the on-ground wheel direction, by player preference) - so the player can set up a landing. This applies a torque to the chassis, capped at a maximum rotation speed of about one turn per second, and does not spin the wheels. Normal drive resumes as soon as a wheel touches the ground.
2.6b. Acceleration: drive power is not instant. When a key is pressed the throttle starts at zero and builds to full power at a rate set by the Acceleration slider (default 8 per second, so full power after about 0.125 s; at 1.5 it takes about 0.7 s). Releasing the key, or changing direction, resets the throttle to zero. Wheel spin also stops building once it reaches the Top speed setting.
2.6c. The Air spin slider scales and aims the mid-air rotation of 2.6a. At +1 the anti-clockwise key rotates the car anti-clockwise and the clockwise key clockwise; at -1 (the default, as the direction was originally flipped by player preference) it is reversed; at 0 mid-air rotation is off; other values scale the torque and the maximum rotation speed.
2.7. When a car's keys are not pressed its wheels roll freely apart from a small rolling resistance (the Rolling resistance slider, default 0.004), so a car on a slope rolls down it. Turning the slider up acts like a handbrake that holds the car in place.
2.8. Controls are ignored during the "Get ready!" pause and after a round has ended.
2.10. Game loop: when the page loads, a start screen shows an "ISAAC'S CAR GAME" logo and four large buttons: "Two Players" (one keyboard), and "Play the Computer" at easy, medium or hard (you drive blue with Z and X; the computer drives red, the red score label reads "Red (computer)" and the help line says the computer drives red). Clicking a button hides the screen, resets the scores to 0 - 0 and starts the first round. The game stands still behind the start screen.
2.10a. A match is first to 5 points. When a player reaches 5, that round's banner says they won the match, and after the usual end-of-round fade the start screen comes back, headed "<player> wins the match!" with the final score, and the four buttons for another game. Draws give no points. There is no Game dropdown any more.
2.11. The computer works the same controls as a person: every 90 to 380 ms (by level) it picks anti-clockwise, nothing or clockwise, and the same torque, acceleration and physics apply, so it does not cheat. It heads for the blue car (medium and hard also detour for nearby power-ups), stops or backs off at a cliff or pit unless there is somewhere to land across the gap, eases off when a wheelie or nose-dive is building, levels the car with mid-air rotation, and rocks back and forth to right itself when upside down. Easy pulses the throttle and makes random choices about 12% of the time; medium holds the throttle 80% of the time with 4% mistakes; hard holds it fully with no mistakes.
2.9. The browser's default action for these four keys is suppressed. All held keys are cleared when the window loses focus.

## 3. The Cars

3.1. There are two cars, seen from the side: one blue and one red.
3.2. Each car has an open-topped sports-car body (cockpit with no roof), a windshield, a racing stripe, a headlight and an exhaust.
3.3. Each car has a front wheel and a rear wheel. The wheels rotate and show their rotation with visible spokes.
3.4. Each car has a passenger of which only the head is visible, wearing a cap in the car's colour. The head is a circular physics shape of radius 10 px.
3.5. The wheels are attached to the car body by rigid axle pins. A car body can rotate about its centre of gravity under gravity, collisions and wheel forces.
3.6. The parts of one car never collide with each other. They do collide with the terrain and with the other car.
3.7. The blue car faces right. The red car faces left (mirrored). The mirroring is visual and physical: the head, windshield and wheel layout are mirrored.
3.10. Bonnet and boot: the front and rear of the body rise above the tops of the wheels (the body top is 16 px above the design centre, the wheel tops 2 px below it), and these are real collision shapes. A wheel never protrudes above the body, so an upside-down car rests on its bonnet, boot and passenger's head, never on its wheels, and spinning the wheels cannot drive it along. The wheels are drawn behind the body so the bonnet and boot cover their upper parts.
3.9. Bouncy tyres: when a wheel lands on the terrain, the car's speed into the surface is reflected, scaled by the Tyre bounce setting (default 0.3; 0 gives no rebound, 1 is perfectly elastic), for all three of its bodies together, so landings rebound. The speed along the surface is kept. Impacts slower than a small threshold (6 px per 16.7 ms) do not bounce, so driving over bumps stays smooth. (Matter.js's own restitution setting was not enough because the axles absorb it.) Matter.js reports body velocity in the wrong units inside a collision event (the raw per-step value, half of what `setVelocity` expects), so the bounce reads velocities with `getVelocity`; an earlier version did not, which made the bounce half as strong as the setting and halved the car's forward speed on every landing.
3.8. Physical properties: wheel radius 22 px (axles 20 px below the car's design centre); chassis 104 x 20 px plus a bonnet and a boot (each 32 x 26 px) at the front and rear; the wheels (centres 44 px fore and aft of the middle) stick out past the front and rear of the body, so nothing on the car extends further forward or back than the wheels; very high wheel friction (2.5) so wheels grip; light head and heavier chassis and wheels.

3.11. Passenger faces: the passenger's face changes with what is happening. Normally the eye is a dot with a small smile. After more than 0.3 seconds in the air, the eyes go wide (a big white eye with a pupil, a raised brow and an open mouth). After a hard landing, and whenever the head has been bumped, the face grimaces for 0.7 seconds (a squinting eye, a scowling brow and gritted teeth).
3.12. Suspension: the axles are springs rather than rigid pins, so a car squashes when it lands (the wheels move up into the body, which hides their tops) and springs back. The Suspension slider runs from 0 (very soft) to 1 (rigid); the default is 0.37, which maps to a constraint stiffness of about 0.03 (the slider maps 0 to 0.004 and 1 to 1, exponentially, because Matter.js solves each axle many times per step and only a very low stiffness gives visible give). Axle damping is 0.2. The axles are rigid whenever the car is upside down (otherwise the wheels could sag onto the ground), and only springy the right way up.
3.13. Special rounds: every 3 to 5 rounds (random) a special round is played, chosen at random from twelve, and announced under the level name when the round starts: MONSTER TRUCK WHEELS (both cars get wheels 1.45 times the radius), TINY WHEELS (0.65 times), MOON GRAVITY (gravity at 40%, so cars float and jumps are huge), GALE FORCE WIND (a steady, gusting sideways push on both cars, with the direction shown as >>> or <<< and white gust streaks drawn across the screen) and SLIPPERY STORM (all ordinary ground gets slick, with friction 0.3 instead of the normal grip, plus falling rain and a darkened sky) and GIANT HEADS (both passengers' heads are 3 times the normal size, 30 px in radius instead of 10, sitting on the same seat so the bottom of the head stays put. The bigger head is a real collision shape, so it is much easier to bump, and it touches the water 20 px sooner. It weighs only twice a normal head, so the car is not made hopelessly top-heavy. The face, cap and shield bubble are scaled to match. Because the tall head can poke into things above a start position, a round is occasionally rebuilt by the usual overlap safety net). For the wheel rounds, wheel mass stays the same whatever the size, drive torque is scaled by the square of the size, the axles are placed so the wheel tops always stay below the bonnet and boot, and cars spawn at the correct height. Gravity returns to normal for the next round.
3.14. Dust and skid marks: where tyres bite, spin or slide, they kick up puffs of dust (white snow on ice, dark flecks on mud, tan dust elsewhere). Hard spin (slip, the difference between wheel surface speed and ground speed, above 9) also leaves a dark skid mark on the ground that fades over 8 seconds (at most 700 marks are kept). A car going over 7 px per step also trails a little dust. Heavy landings throw a bigger burst of dust that grows with the impact.
3.15. Speed lines: above a speed of 10.5 px per step a car leaves thin white streaks hanging in the air behind it; they get longer and more numerous the faster it goes and fade in under a third of a second.

3.16. TOWING CARAVANS (special round): each car tows a caravan (a cream box on two small wheels with a window, a door and a stripe in the car's colour) behind it on a stiff tow bar. The caravan swings on its hitch and weighs about half as much as the car, so it drags a little. It breaks off easily, in any of three ways: the car comes down from a real jump or drop (it must have been fully airborne for at least 0.15 seconds, so a wheelie slapping back down does not count) with a landing impact over 6.5 (a drop of roughly 70 px); the caravan takes a knock whose speed along the contact is over 6.5 px per 16.7 ms; or the tow bar is stretched more than 12 px beyond its length. Ordinary driving, small hops of about 30 px and normal bumps do not break it. When it breaks off, the tow bar snaps ("SNAP!") and the caravan EXPLODES: it is blown to bits and removed, with a blast flash, sparks, dust, "BOOM!", a boom sound and a screen shake. The blast throws any car within 190 px away (the owner's car included, usually forward), at 70% of a bomb's strength, and a head within 60 px of it is blown up (the other player's head if it is right next to the caravan; the owner's own head is normally too far away). A caravan never collides with its own car, but it can hit the other car, and a caravan hitting a head is a head bump.
3.17. TEENSY CARS (special round): both cars, heads, wheels and all, are half size (a quarter of the mass). Drive torque is scaled so the wheelie still takes roughly the normal time, and the engine note is higher pitched.
3.18. BOMB TRAILERS (special round): each car tows a flatbed trailer carrying a black bomb with a fuse. The bomb sits loose in the flatbed, between two end walls 28 px high, and simply rolls and bounces with the trailer. When it bounces out (leaves the flatbed by more than 62 px along it, rises more than 34 px above it, or drops more than 45 px below it), the fuse is lit: the bomb flashes red (faster in the last second), a countdown 3, 2, 1 is drawn over it, and it ticks each second. Three seconds after it was lit it explodes: a blast flash, sparks, dust, "BOOM!" and a big screen shake. Any car within 240 px is thrown away from the blast, harder the nearer it is (a kick of 4 to 22 px per 16.7 ms, with a slight upward bias). A head within 100 px of the blast (plus the head's own radius) is blown up and that player loses ("Blue was blown up"). Both cars have a bomb, so either, both or neither may go off in a round. The bomb stays on its trailer for ordinary driving about half the time over a few seconds of hard driving.
3.19. SQUARE WHEELS (special round): the wheels are squares (rotating, with slightly rounded corners and four bolts) instead of circles, with the same mass and grip. The car lurches and bounces along; the axles are placed so the corners still stay below the bonnet. They drive and launch the car much more bumpily than round ones.

3.20. GIANT CARS (special round): both cars, heads, wheels and all, are twice normal size (four times the mass and over twice the length). Drive torque is scaled so the wheelie still takes about the normal time, the engine note is lower, and power-ups are picked up from twice as far away. To make room, spawn spots, start slabs (380-460 px long) and ring start positions take the car's size into account, and on a curved wall the car starts a few pixels higher so its wheels clear the curve and then settles; on the smaller levels this means more quiet rebuilds.
3.21. Cars face the way they are going: the car is the same shape either way round (the passenger sits in the middle), so facing is purely how it is drawn. A car turns to face the direction the driver is pressing (the clockwise key faces it towards the clockwise-rolling side, the anti-clockwise key the other way); with no key pressed it faces the way it is rolling along its own axis (over about 3 px per 16.7 ms). The turn is instant: the car simply swaps to face the other way, with no rotation or flip animation (an earlier version animated a half-turn, which looked odd). At the start of a round blue faces right and red faces left, towards each other. In the caravan and bomb rounds cars do not turn round, because the trailer is fixed behind them. The windscreen, headlight, bonnet, the passenger's face and the boost flame all follow the facing.
3.22. BENDY THREE-WHEELERS (special round): each car is a long, two-part car (200 px long, 12.3 mass) with three wheels in a line: one at each end and one over the middle, where a hinge joins the two halves. The halves are mirror images, so the car is the same either way round, and the passenger rides over the joint. Each wheel is driven, and each pushes on the half it is mounted on (the rear and middle wheels on the rear half, the front wheel on the front half). A spring at the joint keeps it nearly straight but lets it bend: it bends about 0.03 rad on flat ground, up to about 0.2 rad after a big landing, and about 0.3 rad climbing or driving hard over bumps; past 0.35 rad (about 20 degrees) a much stiffer spring and damping stop it. The two halves are drawn separately so the bend is visible, with a dark joint cover over the middle; the nose half has the windscreen and headlight and the other half the exhaust, swapping when the car turns round. The wheelie is much harder to get than in a normal car (about 1 to 1.8 seconds, or not at all) because the car is long and heavy, and mid-air rotation turns both halves together. Spawn spots take the car's longer length into account (wheels at 84 px, ends at 100 px), start slabs are 300-380 px long, and the same overlap safety net applies.
## 4. The Arena and Levels

4.1. Gravity acts on everything, so cars fall when not supported by terrain. Gravity defaults to 35% stronger than the Matter.js default (scale 0.00135 instead of 0.001), so the cars feel heavy. It can be changed live (see 8.6).
4.2. All terrain is solid and has high friction (2.5). Static terrain never moves. Terrain is drawn as brown ground with a green grass edge. The 1280 x 720 arena always has water along the bottom (see 4.20) and a sky-gradient background.
4.3. Every round is played on a randomly chosen level. Each level type is a basic idea whose details are randomised every time it is built, so no two rounds are alike.
4.4. A level type is never played twice in a row. Each new round picks randomly from the other 10 types. (The "Next" menu in the tune-up panel can force a type for testing; that choice is exempt from the rule.)
4.5. If a level cannot be built with valid start positions, another is tried, up to 15 times, then the simplest (The Bowl) is used.
4.6. The level types are:
  1. **The Bowl** - one continuous, closed, undulating ramp circling the whole screen (an ellipse with three random sine-wave undulations; cars drive on its inside) plus a random boxy island in the middle. Cars start on the lower left and right sides of the ring. Variation: ring shape and island size and position.
  2. **Bowl & Launch Pads** - the same bowl and island, but each car starts on its own random slab platform (200-300 px, tilted up to about 11 degrees; blue left, red right). Start platforms only exist on levels like this one.
  3. **Twin Islands** - two large floating islands (one left, one right, at least 140 px apart) with random size, boxiness and height, and a 45% chance of a small stepping-stone island between them. Cars start on their own island.
  4. **Long Island** - one long island (760-1000 px), slightly tilted, with a 50% chance of one or two small islets floating above its middle. Cars start at opposite ends.
  5. **Rotor Bowl** - the bowl (with a 50% chance of a small island) plus two or three spinning bars in the middle. Cars start on the ring.
  6. **Rotor Bridge** - a start slab on each side with one to three spinning bars or crosses in between to hop across over the water.
  7. **Scatter** - two start slabs plus three to five random flat and sloped slabs, kept at least 90 px apart.
  8. **The Valley** - a flat shoulder at each side (the outer 20% of the screen), with a deep valley between them (250-360 px deep, with a few gentle bumps), open at the top, sometimes with a floating island above its middle. Cars start on the flat shoulders.
  9. **Ramps & Gap** - two landmasses with curved launch ramps and a water gap of 240-380 px between them, sometimes with a floating slab above the gap. The ramps are gentle enough to climb: each is 240-340 px long (or shorter if the landmass is narrow, always leaving at least 260 px of flat ground behind it) and rises 22% to 34% of its length, so the slope at the lip is only about 25 to 38 degrees. Cars start on the flat parts.
  10. **Windmill** - a small island at each side and a large spinning windmill (a bar or a cross, 280-380 px) between them.
  11. **The Circle** - a perfectly round arena (322 px radius, centred on the screen, its bottom just above the water) and nothing else: no island, no floor, walls, power-ups, moving or crumbling platforms, or ice and mud patches. The cars start on the lower left and right of the curve and roll down towards each other; special rounds can still happen in it.
4.7. Curved surfaces are built from many connected straight segments (the ring from 200; islands from 48 or more, scaled to their size; land from segments 16 px wide), each a thin static rectangle whose surface edge lies on the curve, so surfaces are smooth and continuous.
4.8. Rotating platforms spin at a constant random speed of 0.5-1.3 radians per second, in a random direction. They are very heavy bodies pinned to their pivot every physics step, so they carry cars with friction and swat them away. Their positions are random but they keep clear of terrain, start positions and each other. They are drawn as red and white striped bars with a hub.
4.9. Cars follow the terrain and obey physics: they rotate to match the surface they sit on, and they roll, tip, flip, jump and fall.
4.10. In open levels, falling into the water loses and leaving the screen loses (see section 6). In the bowl levels the ring encloses the arena, so only the water and head collisions are likely.
4.11. Dynamic zoom camera (experimental): the view is a 16:9 window onto the 1280 x 720 arena and never shows more than the whole arena, so no space outside it is wasted.
4.12. Each round starts fully zoomed out, showing the whole arena.
4.13. The camera follows the midpoint between the two cars and zooms in (up to 2.4x) to frame them with a generous margin. Zooming in is slow (time constant about 2 seconds); zooming out is fast (about 0.17 seconds), so the camera backs off rapidly when the cars separate.
4.14. Hard rule: both cars are always inside the view with at least a 70 px margin, whatever the smoothing is doing. The camera is clamped to the arena edges.
4.15. When a round ends, the camera zooms back out to the whole arena so the celebration is visible. The scoreboard, banner and hints are not zoomed.
4.16. The camera settings are in a `CAM` object in the page.
4.17. The level name is shown under "Get ready!" at the start of each round and in the tune-up panel.
4.18. Cars in the water experience drag, which slows them.
4.19. Water is drawn on top of the cars with an animated wavy surface. In the bowl levels it only shows inside the ring, so low parts of the ring flood where they dip below the water line.
4.20. Water runs along the whole bottom of the screen, starting 50 px above the bottom edge.

4.21. Floors and side walls: so that falling is not always instant defeat, most levels have a floor and many have side walls.
4.22. Floor: a solid floor runs across the bottom of the arena, in one of three random styles: flat, rolling hills, or flat with one or two water pits (dips below the water line, drivable but cars that flip in them lose to the water). The floor top is kept at least 585 px down so there is plenty of room above it. The open levels (Twin Islands, Long Island, Rotor Bridge, Scatter, Windmill) get a floor 80% of the time, except Rotor Bridge and Windmill, which always have an undulating (rolling hills) floor and keep their spinning bars a little higher so they clear it. The bowl levels are already enclosed, and The Valley and Ramps & Gap have their own ground, so they get no extra floor.
4.23. Side walls: solid walls along the left and right edges so cars cannot drive or fly off the sides. They appear 50% of the time on the open levels and 60% of the time on The Valley and Ramps & Gap. The bowl levels do not need them.
4.24. Floors and walls are placed before the rest of the level, so islands, slabs and rotating platforms keep clear of them. Start positions on Ramps & Gap and The Valley are kept at least 100 px from the screen edge so that cars do not start inside a wall.
4.25. The banner at the start of a round lists any extras after the level name (for example "Scatter - floor with water pits, side walls").

4.26. Surface patches: ground can have raised strips (7 px high, tapered ends, 110-190 px long) of two kinds (ice and mud), placed on straight stretches of ground (slabs, floors and land). About a third of rounds have at least one: none, one, two or three are placed (25%, 35%, 25%, 15%) on distinct stretches at least 170 px from both start positions. The bowl levels (ring) get none. The start-of-round banner names the kinds present.
4.27. Ice patch (pale blue with white glints): friction almost zero (0.004), so wheels spin at full speed and the car barely moves. Wheels have a tiny static friction of their own so that this works; ordinary terrain supplies its own, so normal grip is unchanged.
4.28. Sticky mud (dark brown with bumps): extra grip but heavy drag. While any wheel is on mud the car loses 1.5% of its speed per physics step and the wheels lose 4% of their spin, and the tyres throw mud.
4.29. (Removed.) The trampoline pads that used to launch cars were taken out at the player's request; ice and mud are the only surface patches.
4.30. Sudden death: if a round lasts 35 seconds, sudden death begins and cannot be switched off for that round. It is one of two things, chosen at random: the water rises at 14 px per second (down to a minimum height of 60 px from the top), or two walls close in from the sides at 16 px per second each (until they are 440 px in). A big red "SUDDEN DEATH!" banner with a description is shown for 2.8 seconds, and the screen edges pulse red for the rest of the round. Closing walls are heavy bodies moved every step, so they push cars; a head touching a wall loses as usual, and a head under the rising water loses.
4.31. Screen shake: heavy landings and head bumps shake the screen. The shake decays within about a third of a second.

4.32. Occasional extras. Beyond the level types, some rounds get extra features. They are deliberately infrequent so they stay special, and are listed after the level name in the banner.
4.33. Power-ups (about 30% of rounds): two to four glowing orbs float in open air above the ground, at least 170 px from the start positions and 200 px from each other. A car takes one by driving or flying through it, and a taken orb does not come back that round. Boost (orange, lightning bolt): an instant burst of speed along the car's nose, then for 2.5 seconds 1.8 times the drive torque and 1.4 times the top wheel speed, with a jet of flame from the back. Shield (blue, shield symbol): a bubble round the head that absorbs the next head bump (the bubble pops with a puff and a small shake). After a shield pops there is a 1.2 second grace period in which head contacts are ignored; if the head is still touching something after that, the car loses as usual. A shield does not protect against water.
4.34. (Removed.) The pinball bumpers were taken out at the player's request.
4.35. Moving platforms (about 30% of Twin Islands, Long Island, Rotor Bridge and Scatter, and 25% of Windmill): one or two steel slabs with hazard stripes (150-210 px long) that slide sideways (60% of them, 90-160 px) or bob up and down (60-110 px) on a smooth sine path, at a random speed. They are heavy bodies placed and given their velocity every physics step, so they carry a car riding on them. The whole path is kept clear of ground, island, rotors and start positions.
4.36. Crumbling slabs (about a third of Scatter rounds): one or two of the extra slabs (never a start slab) are crumbling, drawn lighter with cracks. When a car touches one it shakes and glows orange for 0.8 seconds, then drops away under gravity and is removed once it has fallen out of sight.

## 5. Starting Positions

5.1. Blue always starts on the left side of the level and red on the right, so blue tends to start left and red tends to start right.
5.2. Where a car starts depends on the level type (a ring side, a start slab, an island or pillar top, flat land, or a valley slope), with a random position within the allowed area. Start spots on the ring and valley are limited to gentle slopes so cars do not slide away.
5.3. A car is spawned aligned with the surface slope, with its wheels just clear of it, and settles under gravity.
5.4. Start positions have open space above them. Rotors are never placed near a start position.
5.5. A car never starts inside, or touching, the ground. When a start spot is chosen on an island top or on land, the whole car must fit: both wheels must be at least a wheel radius plus 2 px from the ground, and the nose and tail corners at least 3 px, however the ground bumps and curves. On an island, the air just above the chosen point must also be outside the island (this rules out the island's underside; an earlier check that looked for points above the spot could miss it on a long island with sparse outline points, so that car started inside the island).
5.6b. In the giant-car round the whole-car footprint check (both wheels and the nose and tail corners) uses the car's scaled size, so cars are only started where they fit.
5.6a. In the caravan and bomb rounds, spawn spots are also chosen so that the trailer behind the car (about 180 px long) has ground under it: the trailer wheels must be resting on the ground and the box clear of it, checked against the same island, land or ring. On ring levels the spawn is nearer the flatter bottom, and a start slab is 320-400 px long with the car towards the end away from its trailer. If a trailer would hang over an edge or start inside the ground, the level is rebuilt (up to 30 tries in these rounds, about one rebuild per round on average).
5.6. As a final safety net, once both cars are built for a round, every part of both is tested for overlap with any ground, island, platform or rotor. If anything overlaps, the same level type is rebuilt with new random details (up to 8 tries), and the rejected attempt does not count as the previous level for the no-repeat rule. This is needed on only about 1% of rounds.

## 6. Rules: Winning and Losing

6.1. A player loses the round if their car goes off the top, bottom, left or right of the screen. This is judged by the car body's centre. (The ring now encloses the arena, so this is rarely possible.)
6.2. A player loses the round if their passenger's head touches the water.
6.3. A player loses the round if their passenger's head collides with anything: the terrain, the other car, or the other car's wheels.
6.3a. When a head collision causes a loss, a burst of sparks (about 55 yellow, orange and white streaks that fly out, fall under gravity and fade over roughly 0.6-1.5 seconds) plus a brief bright flash comes from the damaged player's head, which then keeps showering sparks for about 1.4 seconds, and that head turns red to show the damage. The head stays red until the next round.
6.4. When one player loses, the other player wins the round and gains one point.
6.5. If both players lose in the same instant, the round is a draw and neither scores.
6.6. Losing conditions are only evaluated while a round is in play (not during "Get ready!" or the end-of-round pause).

## 7. Round Flow

7.1. The game starts a round immediately on page load.
7.2. Each round begins with a "Get ready!" pause of 1.2 seconds. The layout and cars are visible but physics is frozen.
7.3. After the pause, physics runs and the players can drive.
7.4. When a round ends (a knockout), the game plays a short sequence timed from the moment of the knockout. There is no replay.
7.4a. Aftermath (1.5 seconds): the game keeps running live and the banner shows the result (for example "Blue wins!" with the reason, such as "Red's head touched the water"), or "Draw!". If the knockout was a head bump, it plays in slow motion: the game starts at 20% speed for the first half of the 1.5 seconds and eases back to normal, particles slow with it, and the camera punches in to 2.2x on the bumped head. Other knockouts (water, leaving the screen) are not slowed.
7.4b. Fade (1.5 seconds): if one player won, the whole game screen fades to the winner's colour (blue or red) over about 1.2 seconds, up to 80% opacity, and holds. The "wins!" banner is drawn in white on top so it stays readable. A draw has no colour fade. The game keeps running live.
7.4c. At the start of the next round the screen fades back from the winner's colour to normal over about 0.7 seconds instead of cutting.
7.5. When the fade ends (3 seconds after the knockout), a new round starts with a new random layout. Cars are respawned.
7.6. The game continues indefinitely. There is no match limit or overall winner.

## 8. User Interface

8.1. A scoreboard above the canvas shows `Blue 0 - 0 Red`, starting at 0 - 0. Blue is shown in blue and red in red.
8.2. The scoreboard updates immediately when a point is scored.
8.3. A line of control hints below the canvas lists the keys for both players.
8.4. Banner text ("Get ready!", result text) is drawn large and centred near the top of the canvas.
8.5. The page has a dark background and fills the browser window without scrolling.

8.6. A tune-up panel below the game has mouse sliders, with live value read-outs: Top speed (maximum wheel spin 0.2 to 1.6), Suspension (0 to 1, see 3.12) and Volume, plus Sound, Reset, Level and Next-level controls. The other settings were tuned by hand and are now fixed in the code with no sliders: gravity 0.95x, tyre bounce 0.39, torque 0.66, acceleration 2.4 per second (2.6b), rolling resistance 0 (2.7) and air spin -2 (2.6c). Changes apply immediately. A Reset button restores the built-in defaults.
8.8. Slider positions are remembered until the player changes them again, including across reloads, other browsers and the http/https port switch. They are saved in two places: in the browser (local storage) and in a file on the server (`CarGame/App_Data/tune.json`, written through a small `/api/tune` endpoint that only accepts numeric values). On load the server copy wins. The file is git-ignored. Only the two slider values (top speed and suspension) are saved; saved values for the removed sliders are ignored (with a units version, currently 2); other tuning values come from the code. Settings saved before the tyre bounce fix have no version, so on load their tyre bounce is halved to keep the car bouncing exactly as it did when it was tuned (for example a saved 0.78 loads as 0.39). The next slider change saves the converted value with the new version.
8.7. The panel also shows the current level name and has a "Next" menu to force the next round's level type (default: Random).

## 9. Physics Behaviour

9.1. Physics runs on a fixed time step of 1/120 second, independent of display frame rate. A frame longer than 50 ms is capped to avoid large jumps.
9.2. The engine uses 10 position iterations, 8 velocity iterations and 12 constraint iterations for stable axles.
9.3. Cars are created rotated to match their spawn slope, with axle offsets rotated to match.
9.5. Units: velocities are in pixels per 16.7 ms (Matter.js's base step), the unit `setVelocity` and `getVelocity` use. Reading `.velocity` is correct between physics steps (so water drag, rolling resistance and mud drag, which run before each step, are right), but inside a collision event it holds the raw per-step value, so code in collision events uses `getVelocity`. Fall impacts are about 6 for a 60 px drop, 9 for 120 px and 14 for 300 px. Landing dust shows from an impact of 5, screen shake from 8 and the grimace from 11.
9.4. Drive torque is 1.3 per wheel (scaled by the throttle) with a maximum wheel spin of 0.8, air rotation torque is 0.525 with a maximum spin of 0.075, and the air delay is 80 ms (Matter.js units). These are held in a single `TUNE` object in the page.

## 10. Out of Scope for Version 1

10.1. No music (sound effects are in, see section 12).
10.2. No touch or gamepad controls.
10.3. No AI opponent, menus, pause, or manual restart.
10.4. No persistent scores. Scores reset when the page is reloaded.
10.5. No offline copy of Matter.js.
10.6. Levels are not weighted; every type is equally likely (apart from never repeating).

## 12. Sound

12.1. All sound is synthesised in the browser with the Web Audio API; there are no sound files to download. Browsers only allow audio after the player presses a key or clicks, so audio starts on the first key press or click.
12.2. Controls: the tune-up panel has a Volume slider (default 60%) and a "Sound on / Sound off" button, and the M key toggles mute. The volume and mute choice are remembered in the browser. When muted, no sounds are made at all.
12.3. Positional: each sound is panned left or right to where it happens on screen; the blue car's engine is on the left and the red car's on the right.
12.4. Engines: each car has a continuous engine note (a sawtooth and a square wave through a low-pass filter) that rises in pitch and brightness with wheel speed and throttle, and is quiet at idle. Teensy cars buzz at a higher pitch and monster trucks rumble lower. The engines are silent during "Get ready!".
12.5. Effects: a thud on landing that grows with the impact (only for landings of impact 3 or more); a crunch with electrical zaps on a head bump; a splash as a car enters the water; a rising whoosh for a boost pick-up and a three-note bell for a shield; a pop when a shield bursts; a crack when a caravan snaps; a hiss when a bomb is lit, a tick each second (double-speed in the last second) and a deep boom with a rumble when it explodes; a two-note beep when the round starts; a four-note fanfare when a special round begins; a falling two-note "wah" when a player is knocked out and a rising jingle when the winner's colour fade begins; a two-tone siren when sudden death starts; a low rumble when a crumbling slab starts to shake and a crash when it falls.
12.6. Weather beds: during a gale round there is a gusting wind noise; during a slippery storm round there is steady rain.
12.7. Slow motion on a head bump muffles all sound (a low-pass filter closes as the game slows and opens again as it returns to normal speed).
